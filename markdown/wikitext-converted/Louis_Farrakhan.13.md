##  [[Lenore]] guesses INTP 
p. 298: "Another interesting example (of the wholistic and imaginal character of [[Introverted Thinking]]) is the head of the Nation of Islam, Louis Farrakhan. Many of Farrakhan's speeches suggest a classic INTP perspective, in that he believes the structure of American culture deforms and degrades the self-understanding of its Black constituents.  Moreover, his manner of defending his ideas is not analytical but analogical, designed to unify his audience in terms of shared contextual experience.
"Whatever may be said about Farrakhan's views or politics, his process of thought tells us something about [[Introverted Thinking]]'s prophetic power.  At the societal level, this function will bear the burden of moral imperatives a culture has not acknowledged, keeping the outward face of law in touch with the immediate experience of real people."
##  Why not INFJ? 
The above passage is rather surprising.  Wouldn't an emphasis on how people's culture affects their self-perceptions, and an attempt to change those self-perceptions by looking beyond the culturally available options, fit much better [[Lenore]]'s conception of [[Introverted Intuition]], especially when related to secondary [[Extraverted Feeling]]?
So how did [[Lenore]] arrive at INTP?
She seems to emphasize two things: Farrakhan's rhetorical style and an emphasis on first-hand experience.  Of course, she's trying to illustrate [[Introverted Thinking]] here, as an attitude that emphasizes first-hand experience and understanding without regard to shared categories or myths of culture (more than she's offering a type guess).
##  Declaring the social contract null and void 
Much of Farrakhan's rhetoric seems to revolve around the idea that an equitable social contract would give blacks benefits in proportion to what they give, but that actually blacks are propping up the social system without getting much in return, and are therefore not bound by the American social contract.
Why is that not tertiary Ti trying to trump secondary Fe from the "higher" perspective of Ni?
##  Independent perspective vs. first-hand perspective 
One possibility is that [[Lenore]] characterizes [[Introverted Intuition]] in terms of an attitude of _relativity_ of perspectives, especially the attitude of granting the validity of multiple, incompatible viewpoints.  So perhaps she is distinguishing between [[Introverted Thinking]] as leading you to give priority to a perspective derived from first-hand experience and [[Introverted Intuition]] as leading you see things at a second-hand remove, through the intermediary of representations each of which reflects only a part of the whole.
That, in turn, raises the broader question of why so many people who test INTJ seem obsessed with rendering judgements from an _independent_ perspective.  And of course bemoaning the existence of power and power structures that keep people down, especially by setting up a cultural language that encourages some people to see themselves as inferior to others, seems to be an especially common pastime of people who test INFJ.
Wouldn't a great deal of INFJ rhetoric be well characterized as follows? (1) an Ni attitude of seeing biases from an attitude that earnestly attempts to be independent or unbiased, (2) an Fe emphasis on self-definition through social relationship and obligation created by a social contract that you did not originate, (3) a radically Ti perspective of trying to _point out_ (give people first-hand knowledge of) facts that go against or cannot be expressed in terms of the social fictions that keep the society running, and (4) a loathing of Se attitudes that people are to be defined by surface appearances combined with an obsession with the power of surface appearances.
To that, Farrakhan would add an inferior-Se approach of trying to project an image of physical strength, intimidating opponents through viscerally felt fear that he and his followers will "rise up" and destroy their oppressors.  Aren't Farrakhan's attempts to be intimidating, suggesting physical violence without explicitly threatening, typical of tertiary and inferior Se?
##  Inferior Fe? 
Lots of people who test ISTP and INTP seem obsessed with modifying the shared social language so that the things that, in their minds, deserve respect actually get respect.  For example, the [[slashdot http://slashdot.org]] idea that since Linux is the better, more elegant operating system, it and the people who designed it and appreciate it ought to be respected as the best.  This obsession with getting respect, not by earning it according to current criteria by which people really grant respect, but by changing the very ways in which people grant respect, seems classically inferior-Fe.
Could Farrakhan's rhetoric be driven by inferior-Fe in that way?
Here are two reasons why it doesn't quite fit.
(1) Farrakhan is not obsessed with getting respect for himself or for blacks in white people's eyes.  He is not trying to change the cultural language of respect, he's trying to convince the disrespected to see beyond that language and stop playing along with it.
(2) The Linux-geek argument is concerned primarily with the _intrinsic_ superiority of Linux, by virtue of its purely internal elegance.  This internal elegance is the guiding light.  The overriding moral imperative is to mold your mind to match the true way that things fit together, no matter how difficult or time-consuming this is.  The reason Linux has no mass-market appeal is because it requires such a huge investment in time to understand and appreciate it _on its own terms._  That's the usual ITP argument: people should respect the elegant because it's elegant.  Or in other words, the cultural language of Fe ought to be redefined to honor the things I've discovered through relentless Ti refinement in one domain.  (See [[Larry Groznic]].)
Farrakhan's argument, rather, concerns _unfairness_, _obligation_, and _lies_.  He accuses white society of being unfair, not living up to its obligations, and spinning a cultural vocabulary of lies.  He offers his ideas as an impersonal or objective account of the situation, but there is nothing impersonal about his subject matter.  It is all and entirely the ways in which people enter into mutual obligation and accusations that some have failed to live up to those obligations.
##  A wild hypothesis to explain the INTP/INFJ connection 
Here's yet another take on all this.  There's widely observed anecdotal evidence that people who test INTP and people who test INFJ often have a natural rapport.  Some do and some don't, of course, but INTPs often report that many INFJs actually can understand and intelligently think about and apply the ideas they offer and explain through their crazy [[Holistic Spiraling]] rhetoric.  They communicate on the same wavelength.
Hypothesis: the INTPs and INFJs who have this rapport might actually both be Lenore-INTPs, some of whom come out INFJ on type tests because they're friendly people who like to keep their lives organized.  The INFJs with whom typical INTPs don't have that rapport would be actual Lenore-INFJs: they're the ones who regularly accuse INTPs of trying to set themselves up as sole authorities, hear all lines of reasoning strictly through the filter of "Ignoring all the content, what kind of relationship is this person trying to establish?", etc.  (See the [[Rhetorical Clash]] described on [[Introverted Perception]].)
##  Hypothesis: ENTJ 
Yet another angle on Farrakhan may be that he's an ENTJ of the "cult leader" variety.
Notice that even though his rhetoric is somewhat complex, he offers _facts_ as _definitively_ proving his conclusions.  Here was the social contract, here are facts about how it was violated, therefore we have been cheated, _quod erat demonstrandum._
INTP rhetoric swirls around, hitting things from many angles, no one fact proving anything.  The "holistic" aspect of it is the way the conclusion seems to arise from many, many angles at once; and the conclusion is not anything definitive or that leads you to take one definite fork of a decision vs. another (Je), but rather the conclusion is itself a theory--that is, a _way of seeing things_ in which every element has a place and all are in logical harmony.  The object of the rhetoric is just to get you to mentally align yourself with an order believed found in reality: to see things a certain way, not to act in a certain way, and the ultimate guiding principle is just logical harmony for its own sake.
Farrakhan's rhetoric, by contrast, follows the pretty typical "hard sell" ENTJ style, where facts are adduced with the idea that they prove the conclusion in a single step without ambiguity, and these conclusions force you to choose new tangible ways of responding now that you see how your present ways of responding are maintaining a bad situation.
Notice also Farrakhan's relentless repetitiousness.  That also seems to fit with ENTJs and not INTPs.  ENTJs with a calling to spread some gospel will often recite the same principle or argument thousands of times, because they see it as the straightforward cognitive move that gets you from where you are to where they want you to be.  From the Te perspective, fact _F_ proves conclusion _C,_ end of story, so why mess with it?  INTPs who want to spread an idea will more typically improvise new rhetoric each time, to fit their audience--sensing, Ne-style, what outside-the-box idea this particular person would find stimulating to their imagination, and, Ti-style, what this person is in tune with and what he isn't.
Isn't that the [[Introverted]] style, to expect that each person needs to understand things in his own unique way, as opposed to the [[Extraverted]] style of being certain that everyone can see and interpret the same things in exactly the same way?  Doesn't Farrakhan's rhetoric lean heavily toward the latter assumption?
Against the INFJ hypothesis, notice that most INFJs, however much they point out hypocrisy and facts that have no place in the social contract, seldom reach the conclusion that the social contract is to be cast off.  The plea is to please, please adhere to our obligations, perhaps modify the social contract to be more inclusive, but don't throw it out, since it's the only way we've got to keep the peace.  Without the social contract, Se demons would be released.  It seems very odd for an INFJ to preach that a society start over from scratch.  Doesn't "classic" INFJ rhetoric suggest that the social contract contains valuable things, and that what we ought to do is live up to what's already there in the tradition?  Didn't [[Martin Luther King]]'s rhetoric generally work that way?
