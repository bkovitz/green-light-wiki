##  [[Lenore]] guesses INTP 
p. 298: "Another interesting example (of the wholistic and imaginal character of [[Introverted Thinking]]) is the head of the Nation of Islam, Louis Farrakhan. Many of Farrakhan's speeches suggest a classic INTP perspective, in that he believes the structure of American culture deforms and degrades the self-understanding of its Black constituents.  Moreover, his manner of defending his ideas is not analytical but analogical, designed to unify his audience in terms of shared contextual experience.
"Whatever may be said about Farrakhan's views or politics, his process of thought tells us something about [[Introverted Thinking]]'s prophetic power.  At the societal level, this function will bear the burden of moral imperatives a culture has not acknowledged, keeping the outward face of law in touch with the immediate experience of real people."
##  Why not INFJ? 
The above passage is rather surprising.  Wouldn't an emphasis on how people's culture affects their self-perceptions, and an attempt to change those self-perceptions by looking beyond the culturally available options, fit much better [[Lenore]]'s conception of [[Introverted Intuition]], especially when related to secondary [[Extraverted Feeling]]?
So how did [[Lenore]] arrive at INTP?
She seems to emphasize two things: Farrakhan's rhetorical style and an emphasis on first-hand experience.  Of course, she's trying to illustrate [[Introverted Thinking]] here, as an attitude that emphasizes first-hand experience and understanding without regard to shared categories or myths of culture (more than she's offering a type guess).
##  Declaring the social contract null and void 
Much of Farrakhan's rhetoric seems to revolve around the idea that an equitable social contract would give blacks benefits in proportion to what they give, but that actually blacks are propping up the social system without getting much in return, and are therefore not bound by the American social contract.
Why is that not tertiary Ti trying to trump secondary Fe from the "higher" perspective of Ni?
##  Independent perspective vs. first-hand perspective 
One possibility is that [[Lenore]] characterizes [[Introverted Intuition]] in terms of an attitude of _relativity_ of perspectives, especially the attitude of granting the validity of multiple, incompatible viewpoints.  So perhaps she is distinguishing between [[Introverted Thinking]] as leading you to give priority to a perspective derived from first-hand experience and [[Introverted Intuition]] as leading you see things at a second-hand remove, through the intermediary of representations each of which reflects only a part of the whole.
That, in turn, raises the broader question of why so many people who test INTJ seem obsessed with rendering judgements from an _independent_ perspective.  And of course bemoaning the existence of power and power structures that keep people down, especially by setting up a cultural language that encourages some people to see themselves as inferior to others, seems to be an especially common pastime of people who test INFJ.
Wouldn't a great deal of INFJ rhetoric be well characterized as follows? (1) an Ni attitude of seeing biases from an attitude that earnestly attempts to be independent or unbiased, (2) an Fe emphasis on self-definition through social relationship and obligation created by a social contract that you did not originate but that still binds you, (3) a radically Ti perspective of trying to _point out_ (give people first-hand knowledge of) facts that go against or cannot be expressed in terms of the social fictions that keep the society running, and (4) a loathing of Se attitudes that people are to be defined by surface appearances combined with an obsession with the power of surface appearances.
To that, Farrakhan would add an inferior-Se approach of trying to project an image of physical strength, intimidating opponents through viscerally felt fear that he and his followers will "rise up" and destroy their oppressors.  Aren't Farrakhan's attempts to be intimidating, suggesting physical violence without explicitly threatening, typical of tertiary and inferior Se?
##  Inferior Fe? 
Lots of people who test ISTP and INTP seem obsessed with modifying the shared social language so that the things that, in their minds, deserve respect actually get respect.  For example, the [[slashdot http://slashdot.org]] idea that since Linux is the better, more elegant operating system, it and the people who designed it and appreciate it ought to be respected as the best.  This obsession with getting respect, not by earning it according to current criteria by which people really grant respect, but by changing the very ways in which people grant respect, seems classically inferior-Fe.
Could Farrakhan's rhetoric be driven by inferior-Fe in that way?
Here are two reasons why it doesn't quite fit.
(1) Farrakhan is not obsessed with getting respect for himself or for blacks in white people's eyes.  He is not trying to change the cultural language of respect, he's trying to convince the disrespected to see beyond that language and stop playing along with it.
(2) The Linux-geek argument is concerned primarily with the _intrinsic_ superiority of Linux, by virtue of its purely internal elegance.  This internal elegance is the guiding light.  The overriding moral imperative is to mold your mind to match the true way that things fit together, no matter how difficult or time-consuming this is.  The reason Linux has no mass-market appeal is because it requires such a huge investment in time to understand and appreciate it _on its own terms._  That's the usual Ti plea: people should get in tune with things on the things' own terms.  And therefore the cultural language of Fe ought to be redefined to honor the things I've discovered through relentless Ti attunement in one domain.  (See [[Larry Groznic]].)
Farrakhan's argument, rather, concerns _unfairness_, _obligation_, and _lies_.  He accuses white society of being unfair, not living up to its obligations, and spinning a deceptive and self-serving cultural vocabulary which clear-minded people must learn to _see through_ (Ni).  He offers his ideas as an impersonal or objective account of the situation, but there is nothing impersonal about his subject matter.  It is all and entirely the ways in which people enter into mutual obligation and accusations that some have failed to live up to those obligations.
##  A wild hypothesis to explain the INTP/INFJ connection 
Here's yet another take on all this.  There's widely observed anecdotal evidence that people who test INTP and people who test INFJ often have a natural rapport.  Some do and some don't, of course, but INTPs often report that many INFJs actually can understand and intelligently think about and apply the ideas they offer and explain through their crazy [[Holistic Spiraling]] rhetoric.  They communicate on the same wavelength.
Hypothesis: the INTPs and INFJs who have this rapport might actually both be Lenore-INTPs, some of whom come out INFJ on type tests because they're friendly people who like to keep their lives organized.  The INFJs with whom typical INTPs don't have that rapport would be actual Lenore-INFJs: they're the ones who regularly accuse INTPs of trying to set themselves up as sole authorities, hear all lines of reasoning strictly through the filter of "Ignoring all the content, what kind of relationship is this person trying to establish?", etc.  (See the [[Rhetorical Clash]] described on [[Introverted Perception]].)
##  Hypothesis: ENTJ 
Yet another angle on Farrakhan may be that he's an ENTJ of the "cult leader" variety.
Notice that even though his rhetoric is somewhat complex, he offers _facts_ as _definitively_ proving his conclusions.  Here was the social contract, here are facts about how it was violated, therefore we have been cheated, _quod erat demonstrandum._
INTP rhetoric swirls around, hitting things from many angles, no one fact proving anything.  The "holistic" aspect of it is the way the conclusion seems to arise from many, many angles at once; and the conclusion is not anything definitive or that leads you to take one definite fork of a decision vs. another (Je).  The conclusion, rather, is a theory--that is, a _way of seeing things_ in which every element has a place and all are in logical harmony.  The object of the rhetoric is just to get you to mentally align yourself with an order believed found in reality: to see things in certain relationships that can only be defined by reference to the things, not to act in a certain way.  The ultimate guiding principle is just logical harmony for its own sake.
Farrakhan's rhetoric, by contrast, follows the pretty typical "hard sell" ENTJ style, where facts are adduced with the idea that they prove the conclusion in a single step without ambiguity, and these conclusions force you to choose new tangible ways of responding now that you see how your present ways of responding are maintaining a bad situation.
Notice also Farrakhan's relentless repetitiousness (not within a speech but across speeches).  That also seems to fit with ENTJs and not INTPs.  ENTJs with a calling to spread some gospel will often recite the same principle or argument thousands of times, because they see it as the straightforward cognitive move that gets you from where you are to where they want you to be.  From the Te perspective, fact _F_ proves conclusion _C,_ end of story, so why mess with it?  INTPs who want to spread an idea will more typically improvise new rhetoric each time, to fit their audience--sensing, Ne-style, what outside-the-box idea this particular person would find stimulating to their imagination, and, Ti-style, what this person is in tune with and what he isn't.
Isn't that the [[Introverted]] style, to expect that each person needs to understand things in his own unique way, as opposed to the [[Extraverted]] style of being certain that everyone can see and interpret the same things in exactly the same way?  Doesn't Farrakhan's rhetoric lean heavily toward the latter assumption?
Against the INFJ hypothesis, notice that most INFJs, however much they point out hypocrisy and facts that have no place in the social contract, seldom reach the conclusion that the social contract is to be cast off.  The plea is to please, please adhere to our obligations, perhaps modify the social contract to be more inclusive, but don't throw it out, since it's the only way we've got to keep the peace.  Without the social contract, Se demons would be released.  It seems very odd for an INFJ to preach that a society start over from scratch.  Doesn't "classic" INFJ rhetoric suggest that the social contract contains valuable things, and that what we ought to do is live up to what's already there in the tradition?  Didn't [[Martin Luther King]]'s rhetoric generally work that way?
## The Numerology Stuff
My knowledge of Farrakhan is pretty vague--mostly consisting of sound-bites aired by Rush Limbaugh and other afternoon KFI talk show hosts in the mid 90's.  But, aren't some of his arguments of this form: If you multiply the number of members on the board of the US Federal Reserve by the height (in feet) of the Washington Memorial, you get *exactly* the number of transatlantic voyages during the US slave trade.  Coincidence?
Of course he's trying to persuade the audience that the US social contract was rotten to its very core, and that reminders of this are everywhere if you just open your eyes.  Or as Paul Shanklin, Rush Limbaugh's chief satirist once put it, if you just order the Million Man Math Made Easy seminar today for only $_19_.95.
This sure doesn't seem like "developed" Ni *OR* Ti rhetoric.  Do ENTJs get this weirded out with bizarre symbolism?

##  "The Purpose of Education" 
A Farrakhan speech from 1993 calling for putting God at the center of education, and teaching self-knowledge, where self-knowledge is especially knowledge of the past accomplishments of your race: http://www.finalcall.com/artman/publish/article_1000.shtml
"One of America’s greatest crimes was and is depriving us totally of the knowledge of self. This is a crime of immeasurable dimension. Having been deprived of the richness of our history deprives us of the springs and motives of human action that would tell us the possibilities that are within us. If you know what your forebears did, then you know the realm of possibility for you. If you are deprived of that history, then you have nothing to connect yourself to as a person. You are left vulnerable to attach yourself to the circumstances you are given, and in a White society everything that is given to us has a Eurocentric perspective that has historically been hostile to Black people."
Isn't the opposition to "attaching yourself to the circumstances you are given" what [[Lenore]] would call a J perspective?
"We as Black people never, therefore, get a root in ourselves, but focus instead on White people—their vision and accomplishments—as the standard by which we judge our possibilities. Further, we are subtly and overtly taught that we can never measure up to them and achieve their level of accomplishments because, by nature, we are inferior beings."
Isn't the emphasis on choosing for yourself what to value and think of yourself a classic Ni perspective, especially secondary Ni?
"A new public school system should relate the curriculum to the self. When we see the curriculum as an outgrowth of self, then we can identify with the curriculum, giving us an incentive to learn. In the Muhammad University of Islam school system, our students’ learning is facilitated because they identify with the subjects. They are taught that they are the subject. They are taught, 'I am chemistry.' Not, 'I am a student of chemistry,' rather, 'I am biology. I am economics. I am history. I am mathematics.' When we relate the forces within self to the forces that lie outside the self, this connects us to subjects we are studying in a manner that we can ultimately master these subjects.
"Our bodies are controlled by the mind. So mastering economics should not be as difficult with this thought in mind. This body takes in and separates what is useful, puts it to productive use, and eliminates what is wasteful. Therefore, this body is economics and since I am the master of this body, I can master economics."
Doesn't this sound like something from EST or the [[Landmark Forum]] (presumably some leading examples of wacked-out ENTJ perspectives)?  Saying strange things to yourself like "I am chemistry" in order to change your perceptions and thereby "master the universe" seems like classic Te/Ni: the mind held independent from reality, choosing categories and concepts to reprogram one's ways of responding to reality, rather than the Ti/Ne approach of building categories and concepts out of patterns found widely throughout reality.  "I am chemistry" is not a discovery about chemistry: it's not a pattern of valences, atomic weights, reaction ratios, or anything found within the subject.  It's a choice of words to impose _onto_ the subject.  Telling yourself that you "are" economics will make it easier to learn economics!!  Isn't this pretty classic ENTJ "cult leader" talk?
Perhaps a heavily [[Extraverted]] perspective is in evidence throughout in the form of viewing the world as a place primarily of negotiation between competing interests.  Other people try to define things to serve their purposes so you've got to define things to serve your purposes or you won't be able to push back.  Farrakhan preaches a classic EJ "stick to your guns" negotiation strategy.
##  Such strange [[sophistry http://greenlightwiki.com/sophistry]] 
Notice the syllogistic [[sophistry http://greenlightwiki.com/sophistry]] in the argument quoted above that starts with "The mind controls the body" and ends with "The mind can master economics."  This is pure word-game reasoning, deductively corralling the conclusion by legalistic/verbal rules that address nothing of the difficulties in learning and understanding economics.
Isn't that kind of sophistry a little incongruous for someone with a developed [[Thinking]] perspective?
##  Hypothesis: INTP as a public service 
Perhaps the essence of [[Lenore]]'s type guess is that Farrakhan's rhetoric encourages people to look outside the man-made law for solutions to their social problems, and to align themselves instead with a transcendent "natural law"--what [[Lenore]] might call "archetypal truths".
The natural law can only be understood through first-hand experience, but it is experienced as manifest in every day and every moment and is thought to be accessible to all rational beings.  The natural law is not a contract to be signed--not a compromise among many people's interests to make social life definable and workable--but a natural order of things that transcends all personal interests.  It is something to conform your personal interests _to,_ not a framework within which to negotiate your personal interests in relation to other people.  Whereas the man-made law is a contingent thing, ever shifting as people negotiate changes to it, the natural law is felt to be a "necessary truth", unchanging in its essence even as it appears in infinitely varied manifestations.  This way of seeing things is the very essence of Ti, and it's how Ti leads one to form a moral code that "claims the whole self", calling you to duties above and beyond any socially agreed-upon moral code.
So Farrakhan the man may well be an ENTJ, in that he's decisive for empirical reasons that are usually straightforward to articulate and defend (dominant Te) and proselytizes for some vision of how, through choice, we can release more of the infinity of the self (Ni).  On this hypothesis, what [[Lenore]] is calling INTP is Farrakhan's role in the public arena, "calling to account" the very social contract that binds us as a society--calling it to account by a higher standard, one that we can all perceive by noticing, for ourselves, the patterns that exist all around us.

Able, perhaps, to see that his arguments do not establish his conclusions in so cut-and-dried a manner?  Able to see beyond interracial conflict to the vast majority of things in life, which cannot be understood in terms of one person shoving his agenda onto another?
What form might [[Developing the Secondary]] take for Farrakhan?
